# Defence rehearsal script (12-15 minutes)

Sylhet Attack - CSE 400 project defence.

Read this once with the game open and click through every cue. Then rehearse it
three times with a timer: the target is 13:30 with two live flights and one
recorded demo, leaving a minute for the examiner's first question. If you go over
14:30, cut the Ring Course segment (section 8) first, then shorten section 4.

---

## 0. Ten minutes before you present

- [ ] `build.bat` runs clean (double-click it; it should end with `Build OK`).
- [ ] The credits page has your real name, ID and supervisor (three constants at
      the top of `src/hud.cpp`), and the game was rebuilt after you edited them.
- [ ] `tools\smoke_test.ps1` reports **10 of 10 screens passed**.
- [ ] `build\video\demo.avi` exists and `tools\verify_video.ps1` says **VALID**.
- [ ] `build\benchmark.txt` and `build\evidence.txt` are open in a second window
      (Alt+Tab), or printed.
- [ ] Charger plugged in, power plan set to High performance, everything else
      closed (browsers, Teams, Steam). This alone is worth several frames a second.
- [ ] Joystick unplugged unless you intend to use it, mouse sensitivity at 1.0.
- [ ] Game at 1280x720, `F11` fullscreen, title screen showing.
- [ ] Backup copy of the whole folder on a USB stick, plus the video.

---

## 1. Opening - 0:00 to 0:40

**On screen:** the title screen with the city behind it.

> "Good morning. This is Sylhet Attack, a flight simulator of our own
> city, written in C++ with OpenGL and freeglut. Every 3D object you are about to
> see - the aircraft, the bridge, the buildings, the traffic - is generated by
> code at start-up. There are no model or image files in the project."

Point at the title, then at the moving city behind it. Do not touch the mouse yet.

---

## 2. What I set out to do - 0:40 to 2:00

Three objectives, say them as three bullets and then move on:

1. **Render a recognisable Sylhet in real time on ordinary hardware** (the target
   machine is a laptop with Intel UHD integrated graphics - no GPU).
2. **A flight model that behaves like an aircraft**, not an arcade game: lift that
   depends on airflow, a stall you can enter, flaps, trim, ground effect.
3. **Something that can actually be demonstrated and measured**: five missions, a
   cinematic demo, and scripts that produce the numbers in this defence.

> "The scope was deliberately a 15 km city, a light aircraft and five things to do
> with it - not a general simulation platform."

---

## 3. Live flight one: the campus - 2:00 to 3:30

**Cue:** click **Free Flight** on the title screen (or press `1`).

The aircraft is parked on the SIU campus flying strip, both zeros in the HUD.

| Do | Say |
| --- | --- |
| Press `E` | "Engine start - the fuel flow and RPM come alive." |
| Hold `W` until about 40% throttle, wait for the take-off roll | "The elevator has no authority at 20 knots; the aircraft needs airspeed before it will fly." |
| Move the mouse gently back to rotate at ~55 knots | "Rotate. The lift line is perpendicular to the airflow, so the aircraft climbs because the wing is doing the work." |
| Climb, turn left over the river | "The mini-map is drawn from the same road and river data the 3D world is built from." |

Say the last line while over the campus building, so the audience sees the campus
and the city appear together.

---

## 4. The flight model - 3:30 to 5:00

Still flying. This is the engineering section; the HUD is your slide.

- **Lift and stall:** "Angle of attack is shown here. Past about 16 degrees the
  wing stalls - watch." Pull the nose up hard, let the stall warning fire, then
  release and let it recover. "It recovers because the stick is released, which
  is what makes it a model and not a script."
- **Trim and hands-off:** press `.` a few times. "Trim sets the power, the
  aircraft settles into a hands-off cruise - check 2 of the harness measures
  90 seconds with no input and no drift beyond a few metres."
- **Flaps:** press `F` to show AUTO / 0 / 50 / 100 on the HUD.
- **Numbers:** "The model is integrated at a fixed 180 Hz substep, so the
  behaviour does not change with the frame rate - harness check 9 compares
  1/180 and 1/60 second steps."

If asked for evidence here, say: "There is a headless harness, eleven numeric
checks, built from the same source - I can show the output."

---

## 5. The cinematic demo - 5:00 to 7:30

**Cue:** press `Esc` to the pause menu, then `Main Menu`, then **Watch the City
Demo** (or `D`).

> "This is the same world, but filmed: a scripted camera on a spline through the
> campus, the rooftops, Keane Bridge, the clock tower, the Mazar, and then down
> runway 05 at Osmani with captions. It is the fastest way to show the content."

Point out the details as they pass: the big **billboards** - the Imran
International Airlines panel beside the strip in the opening shot, the Al-Amin
Enterprises panel on the riverbank as the city tour comes up the Surma, and the
group photograph on the Osmani apron - the white **windsocks** swinging
downwind beside both runways, and the **birds**: white egrets over the water,
crows over the rooftops, pigeons over the bazaar, geese in a V along the river
and kites soaring above.

Talk over the landmarks as they appear. **If the frame rate on the day is below
20 fps, play `build\video\demo.avi` instead** - it was recorded from exactly this
camera at a fixed time step, so it is smooth on any machine. Say so openly: "I
recorded this in the game itself, frame by frame, so the timing is exact."

---

## 5b. The ten-landmark walk-through - 2 minutes, optional but strong

If the examiners want to see every landmark rather than a film, do this instead
of (or after) section 5. It is ten key presses:

Press **N** repeatedly. Each press puts the aircraft a couple of hundred metres
from the next landmark, wings level and pointed at it, with the name on a card:
Keane Bridge, Ali Amjad's Clock Tower, the Mazar, SIU, Osmani, the railway
station, the district stadium, the telecom tower, Kadamtali bus terminal, Shahi
Eidgah. Two seconds per place, saying one sentence about each - what it is, and
what is special about the model (the clock tower's red corrugated cladding and
pyramid roof, the bridge's riveted red-brown trusses, the Eidgah's tiered brick
terraces).

Numbers **1** to **0** jump straight to a specific one if a question goes to a
particular place.

> "The city is deliberately compact - four and a half kilometres across, and
> dense - so that a demonstration never spends time over empty outskirts. These
> landmark models were checked against photographs of the real buildings."

## 5c. If you want to show the combat - 45 seconds

For ground work: **B** drops a bomb, **G** fires a rocket salvo, and the HUD
counts the nine targets - two fuel tanks, the depot, three convoy trucks, two AA
sites and a radar mast. The AA sites fire flak back, and a landmark that takes
enough of it burns.

The quickest route is **Instant Action** on the title screen: you start airborne
over the river with four bandits inbound. Pull the trigger (**Z**) on the pipper,
press **Tab** to pick the next one, and launch a missile (**V**) when the brackets
are on the target. The gunsight, the target brackets and the radar scope are the
three HUD elements to point out - they are drawn from the same world coordinates
as the aircraft, not faked on screen.

Start the **Ring Course** (or the City Tour). Five or six hostile fighters arrive
over the next half minute: they keep station on you, fire cannon bursts and
launch homing missiles. The HUD shows `HOSTILES` and `FLARES`, and a red
**MISSILE INBOUND** banner appears when one is tracking you - press **X** to drop
a flare and break the lock. You carry five.

> "The missions are a contest rather than a sightseeing trip: the fighters are a
> simple pursuit AI with a limited turn rate, their cannon shells are hit-tested
> against the aircraft each step, and the missiles home with a limited turn rate -
> which is exactly why a hard turn or a flare defeats them."

Fly one ring, dodge a missile, then land or let the course finish. Finishing any
mission ends on the results screen, which now presents a **medal** - gold, silver
or bronze by stars - with the score and landing grade. That is the frame worth
leaving on the projector.

## 5c-bis. Sound, if the room can hear it

Everything you hear is generated in code at run time: the engine note rises with
RPM, the cannon thumps, missiles whoosh away, explosions roll and the missile
warning beeps. Press **K** to mute. The same sample-by-sample approach used for
the terrain means the project still ships with no audio files at all.

## 5d. Briefing, difficulty and the debrief

Before each sortie a briefing card lists the mission, the threats and the load-out -
press any key to launch. On the title screen, `[` and `]` cycle Easy / Normal / Ace;
that changes how many bandits come, whether one of them is an ace, and how much
ammunition and how many flares you carry. The debrief after a sortie adds kills,
gun accuracy, ground targets left and the average landmark damage to the medal and
the score - those four numbers are the ones to point at, because they are all
measured by the systems described above.

## 6. How it is built - 7:30 to 9:30

Switch to the slides/second window (`Alt+Tab`). Keep this to two minutes.

- **Geometry:** the world compiles into display lists: 196 city chunks in a
  detailed version, a simplified one, and a roof pass each, plus one list per
  landmark group. Measured: 1,638 buildings, 25,015 boxes, about 366,000
  triangles before culling.
- **Textures:** 105 CC0 photographs from Poly Haven - plaster, paint, concrete,
  brick, tile, timber, bamboo, tin, roof tiles and thatch for the buildings;
  grass, paddy, soil, mud, gravel, sand, rock and river pebbles for the ground;
  five asphalts for the roads; bark and foliage for the trees. Each building
  takes its wall material from a palette that depends on how central it is, and
  each 600 m chunk gets one roof material from a separate pass - that last
  decision is worth about 20% of the frame rate over the city, because it turns
  a texture change per building into one per chunk. Every photo is box-filtered
  to 512 px on load, so the whole set costs 80 MB instead of 578 MB; at 578 MB
  the integrated GPU paged textures mid-flight and the city view stuttered down
  to 2 fps in the worst sample. If the folder is missing the game falls back to
  flat colours and procedural noise - tested and captured in
  `screenshots/phase1/no_assets.png`.
- **One surface is not a photograph:** the river. No CC0 water photograph tiled
  convincingly, so it is generated in code and its texture coordinates are
  scrolled every frame, which is what makes the Surma flow.
- **Performance:** the interesting part. The bottleneck was geometry submission,
  not pixels: the frame rate did not change between 640x360 and 1280x720. So the
  fix was to submit less. Chunked city with distance culling (2,200 m) and a
  second simplified mesh per chunk beyond 900 m. Both measured on the same
  binary by switching each off from the command line:

| Viewpoint | shipped | no culling | no LOD | neither |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Campus strip | **26.5 fps** | 21.5 | 19.0 | 10.2 |
| City at 300 m | **17.6 fps** | 17.4 | 10.8 | 9.8 |

> "The 'neither' column is the pre-optimisation configuration, measured on the
> same binary that produced the 26.5, so the attribution is not a cross-build
> comparison: culling and distance LOD together are worth about 2.7x at the
> campus and 1.8x over the city. The 105 photographic textures cost about 15%
> when I added them; making the city compact took that back, because it is four
> and a half kilometres across instead of eight."

- **HUD and minimap:** a second orthographic pass, no textures, drawn last.
- **Animated details that are not baked:** the windsocks and the birds are drawn
  every frame rather than compiled into display lists, so the socks swing to the
  same wind vector the flight model flies in, and the birds beat their wings,
  face along their flight path and circle their flock centre. Traffic is drawn in
  every mode, which is why birds and windsocks appear on the title screen, in
  photo mode and inside the cinematic demo as well as in the missions.
- **The recording you just saw:** the game renders with a fixed time step while
  recording and writes MJPEG frames into an AVI itself - no screen recorder, so
  the video is identical on every machine.

## 7. Evidence - 9:30 to 11:00

Show `build\evidence.txt` on screen for ten seconds, then the benchmark file.

- 9,289 lines of C++ in `src/`, plus a 285-line test harness.
- **Smoke test:** ten screens, each launched, captured and checked for a
  non-blank frame, plus where each mission starts: 10 of 10.
- **Flight harness:** eleven numbered checks - trimmed flight, control authority,
  stall, take-off roll, coordinated turn, landing, wind, frame-rate independence,
  no NaNs, runway on dry flat ground: all pass, output in
  `build\flight_test_out.txt`.
- **Binary:** 1.4 MB, statically linked, runs from a USB stick with no DLLs to
  install - deliberately, because the lab machines do not have developer tools.

---

## 8. Live flight two: Landing Challenge - 11:00 to 12:30

**Cue:** `Esc` to the menu, **Landing Challenge** (or `5`).

You start on short final, 600 m out at 45 m, gear down, flaps set.

> "This is the mission that punishes you: the sink rate and the touchdown
> vertical speed decide the grade, and the grade feeds the three stars."

Land it, then let the result screen stand while you talk about the rating
(butter / firm / rough / crash), score and stars. If the landing goes wrong, do
not fight it - restart with `R` and explain that the challenge restarts cleanly.

---

## 9. Limitations and future work - 12:30 to 13:30

Be first to name the weaknesses; it is more convincing than being caught by them.

- The water is a generated ripple texture scrolled by a texture matrix, not a
  real water shader: there are no reflections, no depth and no shore foam.
- The detailed ground mesh ends at 3.4 km; beyond that the city sits on a flat
  green plane, which is visible from high altitude.
- Fixed-function OpenGL 1.1: no shaders, no instancing, no shadow maps. The
  shadows are baked decals, not real-time shadow mapping.
- No sound, and no weather beyond a constant wind.
- Next steps, in the order I would do them: a shader path (per-pixel lighting and
  a real water surface), instanced buildings, terrain height LOD, then sound and
  a proper terrain-driven crash model.

## Questions they are likely to ask

| Question | Short answer |
| --- | --- |
| Why freeglut and fixed-function OpenGL? | The course is computer graphics fundamentals; keeping the pipeline old means the projection, lighting maths, display lists and culling are all visible in my own code, and it runs on integrated graphics. A shader port is the first item in future work. |
| Why only 20-30 fps? | It is geometry-bound on an Intel UHD 620 - I measured that resolution does not change the frame rate. Two optimisations (chunk culling and distance LOD) doubled it; further gains need shaders or instancing. |
| How do you know the flight model is right? | Lift acts perpendicular to the airflow, induced drag rises with AoA, the wing stalls past ~16°, and the harness checks eleven behaviours against hand calculations - take-off roll, turn rate, landing, wind drift. |
| What is the frame rate independence check? | The same input sequence is integrated at 1/180 s and 1/60 s; altitude differs by 2.7 m after the manoeuvre, so the model is not tuned to one frame rate. |
| Is the city really Sylhet? | The landmarks are modelled to the real layout and proportions - Keane Bridge, the clock tower, the Mazar, Osmani's runway 05/23 on heading 50°, the SIU campus - and they are placed on a 1:1 map of the roads and the Surma. |
| Did you use any downloaded models or textures? | No models at all. 105 CC0 photographs from Poly Haven for surfaces, all optional: the game falls back to flat colours if the folder is missing. `LICENSES.txt` lists every component and licence, and `assets\textures\_sources.txt` names the Poly Haven asset behind each file. |
| How does the traffic work? | Vehicles follow waypoint loops built from the road polylines, always on the left of the centreline; there are also boats, pedestrians, a train and two AI aircraft in the pattern. |
| How does the HUD get drawn? | A second orthographic projection pass at the end of the frame; values come straight from the flight model, and the numbers use the stroke font so they stay crisp. |
| What was the hardest part? | Making a code-generated city look like a real city and keeping it fast enough; the flight model's low-speed behaviour (elevator authority and ground effect) took the most tuning. |
| Can it run on the lab PCs? | Yes - it is a single 1.4 MB static executable with no installer and no console window. Bring the folder on a USB stick and it runs. |
| What about collision and crash? | Buildings use a list of footprint boxes, the terrain uses the hill height field; hitting either damages the aircraft and the mission ends with a crash rating. |
| Why is there a cinematic demo? | Two reasons: it shows every landmark in under two minutes, and it doubles as the video recording - the demo can be recorded frame by frame at a fixed time step. |
| How long is the source? | 9,289 lines in `src/` across 20 files, plus the tools. `tools\evidence.ps1` prints the breakdown. |
| What would you change with two more months? | Shaders and instancing for the city, real-time water, terrain LOD, and a proper sound layer. |
| Can you show the code of X? | Open the file and jump to the function - know where each of these lives: `craftStep` (aircraft.cpp), `genBuilding` (world.cpp), `worldDrawStatic` (world.cpp), `hudDrawFlight` (hud.cpp), `gameDisplay` (game.cpp), `KEYS` (demo.cpp). |

## If something goes wrong on the day

| Problem | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Frame rate is much worse than at home | Press `Q` (shadows off) and restart with a smaller window: `SylhetAttack.exe --free --size 1024 576`. Power plan to High performance, unplug the second monitor. |
| The live demo stutters | Play `build\video\demo.avi` and keep talking - it is the same demo, recorded on this machine. |
| The game will not start | Check the exe is the 1.4 MB static build (not a debug console build), then run `run.bat`. Worst case, present the video and the evidence files. |
| The mouse will not steer | Left-click once to recentre the stick, or fly with the arrow keys. |
| A mission goes wrong | `R` restarts the current mission; `Esc` returns to the menu; nothing is lost. |
| The projector is 4:3 | Run `SylhetAttack.exe --size 1024 768`; the HUD scales with the window. |
| You are asked something you cannot answer | Say what you measured, what you did not measure, and what you would do next. Do not invent a number. |
